## Introduction

This page is about how I run a Contest scene to resolve a situation in the story 
we are telling. A situation resolvable by a Contest would be when two or more 
sides' goals are in direct opposition but none of the sides' goals is to 
actually hurt the others. The usual examples are a race, a debate, or arguing a 
case before a judge. They may be _willing_ to hurt the other side, but that is 
not necessary to attain their goals.

I am outlining just the simplest case here. There are many more
complicated situations which can be handled by a Contest scene using
modifications of this process. I will make those modifications for those more 
complicated situations as they come up, and try to document them here.

A Contest is a scene broken up into _exchanges_. At the end of each _exchange_,
each side of the Contest makes an _Overcome_ action to determine a number of
successes towards their goal they achieved. When a side reaches the total number
of successes needed to win, then they reach their goal, and the other sides do
not.

In each _exchange_ the characters in the scene may also take a 
_Create an Advantage_ (_CaA_) action to make the _Overcome_ action easier for 
their side. There is a risk associated with taking a _CaA_ action, detailed
below.

## Steps for a Contest

1. ***What are the character's goals?***: 

   For each character, determine what they want to get out of the scene. These 
   are the characters' goals for the scene.

2. ***What are the sides and who is on each side?***:

   Determine the sides of the Contest based on the goals of the characters, by
   grouping the characters together based on common or compatible goals.

3. ***Is the Overcome Action's Opposition Passive or Active?***:

   Is the _Overcome_ roll result compared against a static difficulty or is it 
   compared against each of the results of all the sides of the contest?

4. ***Who goes first?***:

   Determine which character goes first in the contest. This is usually 
   determined based on what makes sense in the fiction.

5. ***Number of successes***:

   Usually three, may go higher if the situation is more complex, or if you want
   to spend more time in the session on the scene.

6. ***Exchanges***:

   All _Create an Advantage_ (_CaA_) actions for all sides in the exchange 
   are resolved before any sides' _Overcome_ action is resolved. Any character
   in the scene may _Defend_ against the _CaA_ action if it makes sense.

   * The players for each side should determine who will make the _Overcome_ 
     action for their side.
   * *CaA*
     * Ask each player if they are going to attempt a _CaA_ action for
       their side in this exchange. (Note risk of failure if any players might
       not be clear on the risk.) All _CaA_ actions happen simultaneously.
     * If a player attempts a _CaA_ action, ask them to narrate their action, 
       using the ellipses trick.
     * Decide on and narrate any _CaA_ actions that NPCs on each side will 
       attempt.
     * Once all characters' _CaA_ actions are narrated, resolve each of them as
       normal. If another character is in position to _Defend_ against the _CaA_
       action, they may do so. Narrate the result for each; remember that the
       all the actions happen simultaneously.
       * If a _CaA_ action fails, their side's _Overcome_ action is forfeited 
         for the exchange. (***Note:*** They may Succeed at a cost by giving an 
         opposing side the free invoke on the Aspect created, allowing their own
         side to roll their _Overcome_ action.)
   * *Overcome*
     * *NOTE* Remember that sides where any _CaA_ action failed forfeits their _Overcome_
        action for the exchange.
     * Ask the _Overcome_ action player for each side to narrate their action. 
     * Characters who can help in the _Overcome_ roll for their side may narrate 
       how they are doing so, per the usual rules about helping. This is in
       addition to successful _CaA_ actions they took in the *CaA exchange*.
     * Resolve the _Overcome_ action for each side for the exchange. The 
       _Overcome_ actions for all sides happen simultaneously.
   * *Count and record successes*
     * If no side's total successes are equal or greater than the number of 
       successes for the Contest, run another exchange.

### Threats in a Contest

It is possible that there are narrative elements in the scene which can cause
harm to the contestants, such as artillery fire, eruptions of lava, a hurricane,
or attacking lava men. These _threats_ do not have any other goal in the scene.

If there are one or more _threats_ in the scene, each threat should be allowed to
act once in each _Overcome action exchange_, after both sides have completed
their _Overcome_ action.

If the _threat_ can make an _Attack_ action against contestants, their player (the
GM) narrates and resolves their _Attack_ action. 

If the _threat_ is passive, i.e. they have a static difficulty rating, then treat
that rating as their _Attack_ action result on their turn in the exchange.

All characters on all sides whose _Overcome_ result is lower than the _threat_'s
_Attack_ takes a hit. They must absorb the difference just like in a Conflict. 
If they cannot absorb all the shifts of the hit, they are taken out and can no 
longer act in the scene.
